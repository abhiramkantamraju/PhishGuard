How the score works

PhishGuard has no machine-learning model and nothing hidden: it looks for a fixed set of behaviours, and every point in the score can be traced back to one of them.

One score per category, not per phrase

Phishing emails usually repeat the same idea several ways — "verify your account", "confirm your details", "update your records". Counting each phrase separately would let one theme dominate the score, so each category below adds its points at most once, however many of its phrases matched. The score is therefore closer to a count of independent reasons to distrust an email.

A score below {{ suspicious_threshold }} is reported as Safe, {{ suspicious_threshold }}–{{ dangerous_threshold - 1 }} as Suspicious, and {{ dangerous_threshold }} or more as Dangerous. Those cut-offs were chosen by measuring against thousands of real phishing and real legitimate emails — the reasoning is in the project README.

What it deliberately doesn't do

Two findings are shown but never scored: "this email contains links" and "this email contains an insecure HTTP link". Almost every real email contains a link, so scoring them flagged ordinary mail as phishing — they are context for the reader, not evidence.

PhishGuard also can't see anything it wasn't told to look for. A carefully written phishing email that avoids every phrase in the rule set will score Safe. Treat a low score as "nothing obvious found", never as "this email is genuine".
